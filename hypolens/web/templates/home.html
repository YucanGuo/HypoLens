What is Graph Hypothesis Testing?
🧑‍💼 The decision: A streaming platform analyst needs to identify which audience to recommend Sci-Fi movies to.
“Do students rate Sci-Fi movies more favorably than educators?”
The analyst turns the decision into a testable hypothesis — one involving a graph structure (user–movie viewship), attribute filters (occupation, genre), and a statistical test (a student's rating vs. an educator's rating on the same movie). HypoLens lets you express all of these visually on a pattern canvas, then runs the statistics for you. The loop below traces a single analyst from question to decision.
1
🧑‍💼“Which audience should receive Sci-Fi recommendations?”
Frame the Decision
A domain question that depends on graph structure and attributes — not a flat table filter. The analyst needs evidence, not intuition.
2
🧑‍🎓→🎬“Students who watched Sci-Fi — draw the pattern.”
Build the Pattern
Draw typed nodes, directed edges, and attribute filters on the visual canvas to describe the subgraph you care about.
3
📋TEST mean(⭐ rating₍student₎ − ⭐ rating₍educator₎) > 0
Define the Hypothesis
Pick the target attribute and set the statistical TEST — a node attribute, an edge attribute, or a difference between two nodes or two edges, compared against the population mean or a threshold.
4
🎲Sample subgraphs → significance test
Run Testing
The engine samples the graph, extracts the subgraphs that match your pattern, and applies rigorous significance testing.
5
✓“Accepted — students do rate Sci-Fi higher.”
Read the Results
An LLM-explained summary highlights the testing decision and evidence strength, with technical details folded underneath.
6
🔄“What about 1990s Sci-Fi? Female students?”
Explore Follow-up Hypotheses
The system recommends validated follow-up hypotheses based on your current result — each loads directly into the canvas.
↩ refines the hypothesis, closing the loop  →  📊 “Recommend Sci-Fi to students.”
